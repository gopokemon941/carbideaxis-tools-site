External vs Internal Turning Inserts: What Actually Changes Inside a Bore

The code system is the same inside a bore as outside it — what changes is the room you have to work in. External turning is limited by edge strength; internal turning is limited by the bore itself: its diameter caps the bar and insert size, the bar's overhang invites deflection, and chips have to escape through the hole they were cut in. That is why internal work runs smaller, usually positive inserts — CCMT and DCMT — while external work runs the bigger negative families.

Why does the bore diameter choose the insert, not you?

Outside the workpiece, you pick the insert size your work needs and then the holder that carries it. Inside a bore the order reverses. The boring bar has to enter the hole with room to spare for the insert pocket and for chips to pass, so the bar diameter is set by the smallest bore in the job — and the bar diameter sets the insert size it can carry. Small bars take 06-size inserts (CCMT060204 class), mid-size bars take 09-size (CCMT09T308 class), and only larger bores leave room for 11-size and above (DCMT11T308 class).

Practical consequence: quoting an internal job from the insert code alone is not enough. The same CCMT09T308 fits several bar diameters, but whether it can work in your part depends on your bore diameter and depth. Send both with the inquiry — they are the first two facts an internal quote is checked against, ahead of grade.

Why are internal inserts usually positive?

Three reasons, all forced by the closed space. First, cutting force: a positive insert with a 7° clearance angle and a sharp, raised rake cuts with less force, and less force is what a long, slender bar can tolerate without deflecting. Second, size: positive inserts are made in the small sizes boring bars carry, with a screw hole for the simple screw-down clamping small bars use. Third, chip flow: the positive geometry lifts the chip and helps it curl out of the bore instead of packing against the wall.

The trade is the one covered in negative vs positive inserts: a positive insert cuts on one side only and its thinner, sharper edge is less forgiving of interrupted, scaled surfaces. Inside a bore that trade is usually worth making; outside it often is not.

What goes wrong inside a bore that never happens outside?

  • Chip packing. The chip is born inside a tube and must leave through it. Long, stringy chips wrap the bar or jam between bar and wall, and the next pass recuts them — edges chip for reasons that look like grade problems and are actually evacuation problems. Chipbreaker class matters more inside a bore than almost anywhere else.
  • Deflection. A bar cutting at four or five times its own diameter in overhang is a spring. It pushes away from the cut, so the bore comes out tapered or oversize at the entry and under at depth, and it sings — chatter marks on a bore wall are the bar's signature, not the insert's.
  • Blind sizing. Outside, you can see the edge and the surface. Inside, the first evidence is the measurement after the pass. Trial discipline — one variable at a time, results recorded per edge — pays back faster in boring than in external work.

None of these is solved by a stronger insert. They are solved by the shortest bar that reaches, the largest bar the bore admits, and a chipbreaker that breaks the chip short enough to leave.

Which regular models cover which side?

Of the twelve turning models stocked as standard here, the internal side is carried by the positive families: CCMT060204 for small bores, CCMT09T304 and CCMT09T308 for mid-size bars, DCMT11T308 where the bore admits an 11-size and the 55° point is needed for profiling. The external side is carried by the negative families — CNMG120408 and CNMG120404, WNMG080408 and WNMG080404, TNMG160408, DNMG150608, SNMG120408, VNMG160408. All trial from 10 pieces of one model, with sample and trial orders typically taking 7–15 days. The full table is on the turning inserts page, and the three-way positive comparison — including where TCMT sits, since it is not in the regular range — is in CCMT vs DCMT vs TCMT.

Can one insert do both external and internal work?

Sometimes, and it is the positive inserts that cross over. A CCMT09T308 seats in a screw-down pocket, and that pocket exists on both boring bars and compact external holders — small lathes often run the same insert outside for light turning and inside for boring. The negative families do not cross in the same way: a CNMG needs its 0° pocket and lever or clamp system, and while large boring bars for negative inserts exist, the bores most shops ask about are positive territory.

Crossing over is a fit question before it is a cutting question — the checks are the same three as in how to match an insert to its holder: shape and size code, clearance system, and the seat itself.

What should an internal turning inquiry include?

  • The insert code if known — or the five photos in what photos to send for insert matching, with the bar included.
  • Bore diameter and depth — the two numbers that decide bar and insert size before anything else.
  • The boring bar code or a photo of its marking, if you run one already.
  • Workpiece material — the grade and chipbreaker direction follows the workpiece material groups, and inside a bore the chipbreaker choice carries extra weight.
  • The current problem, if there is one: chatter, taper, chip packing, edge life. Each points at a different first check.

FAQ

Is a boring insert different from a normal turning insert?

Not in its code system. The same ISO letters describe shape, clearance, tolerance and size on both sides of the wall. What differs is which families the work selects: internal work concentrates on small positive inserts because the bore caps the bar size and the bar cannot take external-turning forces.

Why can't I use my CNMG inserts for boring?

In most bores, room. A CNMG120408 needs its 12-size pocket and clamping inside a bar slim enough to enter your bore with chip room to spare; in small and mid-size bores that bar does not exist. Large bores do admit negative-insert bars, but the standard internal range is built on positive inserts.

What is the smallest bore a CCMT060204 works in?

The insert follows the bar: a 06-size insert rides on the smallest boring bars, and the bar maker states a minimum bore diameter for each bar code — the bore must clear the bar, the pocket and the chip path. Send your bore diameter and the bar you plan to use, and the pairing is checked before quoting rather than guessed from the insert size alone.

Why does my bore come out tapered?

The usual cause is bar deflection: the bar bends away from the cutting force, more at full depth than at the entry, so the deep end measures smaller. A shorter or larger bar, a lighter cut per pass, or a sharper positive geometry are the first checks — in that order — before changing the insert grade.

Why do chips keep packing in the bore?

Because they cannot fall away; they have to be carried out. A chip that comes off in long strings wraps the bar or jams against the bore wall. The fix is a chipbreaker class that breaks the chip shorter for your material, checked against your feed — which is why the material group belongs in an internal inquiry even more than an external one.

Do internal inserts have fewer usable edges?

Yes, by construction. The positive inserts used inside bores cut on one side: a CCMT has 2 usable corners, a DCMT 2, and they are not flipped. Negative external inserts cut on both sides — 4 corners on a CNMG, 6 on a WNMG. Cost per edge comparisons have to count the corners actually usable in the holder you run.

Can I trial internal inserts the same way as external ones?

Yes. The regular positive models trial from 10 pieces of one model, and sample and trial orders typically take 7–15 days. Score the trial on bore finish and measured size at depth, not just edge life — inside a bore, the size record is the result.

My boring bar code is worn off. What do I send?

Photograph the bar next to a ruler for its diameter and length, the pocket with and without the insert, and the insert's five views. Bar diameter plus pocket shape identifies the bar family in most cases, and the insert match is confirmed from there before quoting.

Does coolant help inside a bore?

Through-bar coolant is the most effective form because it delivers fluid to the edge and pushes chips out in the same motion. If your bar has no coolant hole, chip control has to come from the chipbreaker and the cut itself — another reason the chipbreaker class is confirmed against your material before an internal quote.

External or internal, the first question is the space

Outside the part, choose for edge strength and cost per corner. Inside it, let the bore choose: bar first, insert size second, geometry third. If the job is internal, send the bore diameter and depth with the code or the photos — those two numbers move an internal inquiry from a guess to a check.
