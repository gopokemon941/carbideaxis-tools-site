MGMN Grooving Inserts: Match the Width, Holder and Job Before You Order

Match groove dimensions, holder seat and cutting conditions before quoting.

Need help matching a tool to your job?Ask about this guide on WhatsApp

“MGMN insert” is a starting description, not a complete order. The buyer still needs to confirm the cutting width, holder seat and planned groove or parting operation. Two inserts that look alike online may differ in size, chipbreaker, grade or the holder system they fit. If you already have a working setup, keep the old insert and holder codes together when requesting a replacement.

Begin with the part drawing

Give the required groove width and tolerance, groove depth and whether the cut is an external groove or a parting operation. If you need an internal groove, flag it separately so the supplier can check the appropriate insert-and-boring-bar system rather than assuming a standard external MGMN setup will fit. If the tool must cut near a shoulder, identify the clearance available on each side. A parting cut toward the center has different chip flow and stability concerns from a shallow external groove. Narrower is not automatically better: the insert must meet the drawing while remaining stable enough for the reach and material. Sandvik Coromant notes that parting and grooving quality depends strongly on tool setup as well as cutting material. Tool setup guide

Check the holder before comparing prices

Read the full code stamped on the holder or blade. Ask the supplier to verify the insert seat, clamping style and insert width against that code. A photo of the holder with the old insert installed often prevents a mistaken “equivalent” quote. Confirm left- or right-hand orientation if your setup requires one.

Overhang matters too. The shortest practical overhang generally improves stability and reduces vibration; a longer reach can make a thin blade or narrow insert more sensitive to deflection. If you are changing the holder as well as the insert, request a matched tool-and-insert proposal rather than shopping the pieces independently. Sandvik Coromant tool setup guide

Material and symptoms guide the edge choice

Tell the supplier whether the workpiece is steel, stainless steel, cast iron or another material, and whether the cut is continuous. Describe the actual failure: chips packing in the groove, chatter, burrs, insert breakage, poor finish or short tool life. These details help distinguish a chip-control problem from a holder or machine-stability problem. The insert grade and geometry should be selected for the workpiece and operation; a code such as MGMN by itself does not answer that choice. Sandvik Coromant parting and grooving white paper

What to include in an MGMN inquiry

Send the drawing or a marked-up photo showing groove width and depth, the current insert and holder codes, workpiece material, current cutting problem and required quantity. If you know the cutting speed, feed, coolant method and machine type, add them. For a repeat purchase, state whether the goal is a direct replacement or a performance improvement; the quotation can then address the correct priority.

For example: “We need an external groove [width] mm wide and [depth] mm deep in [material]. Our current MGMN insert and holder codes are [codes]. Chips are packing in the groove. Please check a compatible insert for a trial of [quantity].” A drawing and holder photo make this much easier to verify than the word “MGMN” alone.
