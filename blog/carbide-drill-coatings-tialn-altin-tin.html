TiAlN vs AlTiN vs TiN: which coating should I choose for carbide drills?

Compare TiN, TiAlN and AlTiN coatings for carbide drills by material, hole depth, coolant and chip evacuation. Learn what to send before requesting a quote.

Quick Answer

TiN is a familiar general-purpose drill coating; TiAlN and AlTiN are aluminum-titanium-nitride coating families often considered where heat and wear are more demanding. The name alone does not identify the best drill. Compare the manufacturer's actual coated grade together with the workpiece, drill geometry, hole depth and coolant method. If you are replacing a drill, send its complete code instead of ordering by coating color.

What can TiN, TiAlN and AlTiN tell a buyer?

The three labels describe coating families, not interchangeable performance grades. Thickness, layer design, substrate and point geometry can differ between suppliers. Use the table as a question list, then check the proposed drill's application data.

Coating familyUseful starting question
TiNIs the existing general-purpose drill working at a moderate thermal load, and is an exact replacement available?
TiAlNDoes the supplier recommend this specific grade for the material and heat generated in the planned cut?
AlTiNWhat does the maker's data say about this grade's drilling application, substrate and coolant conditions?

Guhring describes TiN as a general-purpose drilling coating and TiAlN as a more heat-resistant option for suitable high-temperature work. AlTiN is also used for cutting tools, but it should not be treated as automatically superior to TiAlN. Different manufacturers use different coating architectures and names. A gold or dark surface is not enough to verify the chemistry or suitability.

Put the workpiece before the coating

Send the exact workpiece grade and hardness or heat-treatment condition. Low-alloy steel, austenitic stainless, hardened steel and aluminum do not create the same drilling problem. In stainless steel, chip formation and evacuation can be more important than a simple coating comparison. For aluminum, adhesion and chip flow may make a polished or uncoated design worth reviewing; do not assume a heat-resistant ferrous-metal coating is the right choice. Include whether the hole is in a casting, plate or solid bar and whether the entry or exit is interrupted.

Hole depth and coolant can change the answer

Describe depth as both a measurement and a ratio to diameter, such as 5×D. Note whether the hole is blind or through, the required tolerance and finish, and whether the machine supplies external or through-tool coolant. Deeper holes give chips a longer path out; a coating cannot compensate for poor flute geometry, trapped chips or inadequate coolant delivery. Ask the supplier to confirm the drill design and cutting-data range for your actual depth and coolant setup. Do not transfer speed and feed figures from a different drill solely because its coating has the same name.

What should a drill-coating RFQ include?

  • Full current drill code or drawing, diameter, point style, flute length, overall length and shank size.
  • Workpiece grade and hardness; hole depth, blind or through condition, tolerance and finish target.
  • Machine and holder, coolant method, current speed and feed, plus photos of wear, chipping or packed chips.
  • Trial and repeat-order quantities, delivery location and any packing requirements.
